There are four main areas to provide this information:

Central: matchup breakdown

The central element is the deep dive into a specific matchup, allowing for a comprehensive breakdown of what makes a matchup sway one way and by how much. It shows decision weight, confidence, playstyle counters, and a score of the overall prediction. There are small info buttons that the user will see to elaborate on a metric in a way that isn't invasive on the design, allowing the user to understand exactly how a metric comes to show the decided integer.

Left: matchups by gameweek

The left column shows all the fixtures that are happening for one specific gameweek, allowing the user to understand who is playing who on what specific weeks. The user can also click on a matchup and the matchup breakdown will appear in the middle. This solely aims to provide the user with a different way to explore what's coming up in the season.

Right: fixtures by team

The column on the right shows all the fixtures that are ahead for the chosen team, meaning the user can see how strongly a team fares in its upcoming games. Similarly to the matchups by gameweek, the user can also click matchups to explore a deeper breakdown, and it serves the same purpose of providing alternative ways to filter what comes ahead.

Bottom: full season strip

The bottom strip provides the view with the best horizon, but this took the most work to get right, balancing interaction mechanics, animation, spacing, and graphic indications. This area is designed to highlight the notable matches when considering FPL specifically. It allows a view that highlights strong and weak gameweeks, matches with extreme one-sided predictions, strong captaincy predictions, double gameweeks, postponed fixtures, and suggested best times to use an FPL chip. The work taken to get the animations, spacing, colour coding and methods, general design philosophy, and presenting errors ironed out took roughly a million tokens of repeated tweaks, bug fixing, and testing to get right, but I think it's one of the most useful tools on the site for FPL users specifically.

Scoring philosophy

Every FPL tool gives you the same thing: a 1-to-5 star rating pulled straight from the official app. Gaffer IQ builds its own rating from scratch, and answers a sharper question; not "is this fixture hard," but "is this fixture hard for this specific player, doing this specific job, right now." FPL's rating calls a match "easy" because the opponent's bottom of the table. Gaffer IQ checks whether your striker's opponent specifically has leaky centre-backs, whether that team plays completely differently at home versus away, and whether their rating is outdated and overrated. Several angles, calculated separately, then combined, because "hard fixture" and "hard fixture for a winger" are different questions, and most tools collapse them into one.

The part that makes it trustworthy is how it handles weight and confidence. Not every signal gets an equal vote; recent form and team strength carry real weight; head-to-head history barely moves the needle, because a rivalry from three managers ago isn't strong evidence today. And when the data's too thin (a promoted team with no track record) the model doesn't fake confidence. It knows the difference between "no strong opinion" and "bad fixture," and never confuses the two. Most tools will happily hand you a confident number standing on almost nothing.

Every score also comes with its reasoning attached — you can see exactly what pushed it up or down. It's not "trust us," it's "here's the case." In one line: this isn't a smarter-looking star rating; it's a tool that tells you where an edge is coming from, and how much to believe it.
